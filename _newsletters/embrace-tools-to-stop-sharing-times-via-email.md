Over the past few years, appointment scheduling with people outside your organization has become significantly easier to do as collaboration platforms created functionality that was previously requiring a paid add-on.

Does this drive you nuts?

* Monday 9am to 12pm open, except 11-11:15
* Tuesday 11-11:30, 1-2, 3-5
* Friday unavailable

What about when you receive two or 10 of those and need to figure out which times overlap?

Let’s make a pact that we’ll stop sharing times like that via email.

If you’re using collaboration suites like Google Workspace or Microsoft 365, there are built-in features that are likely included with your subscription at no additional cost:

* **Google Appointment Scheduling:** [marketing page](https://workspace.google.com/resources/appointment-scheduling/), [start here to setup this feature](https://support.google.com/calendar/answer/11608416?hl=en)
* **Microsoft Bookings:** [marketing page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/business/scheduling-and-booking-app), [start here to setup this feature](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/bookings/bookings-overview?view=o365-worldwide#accessing-microsoft-bookings)

Each of those hits a limit in terms of its features and integrations, so you might need to consider a solution outside of your collaboration suite that is an additional cost or bundled into a tool that you already have.

Standalone products like **Calendly** are feature-rich and can scale to handle many different use cases. If you’re dipping your toe into the water but have a wide opportunity space, it’s probably worth going down this path. Here are links to their [marketing page](https://calendly.com/features) and [how to get started](https://help.calendly.com/hc/en-us/categories/23988873731991-Getting-Started).

Video meeting platforms like Zoom and CRM platforms like Salesforce and HubSpot have bundled this feature into certain tiers of their products.

* **Zoom Scheduler:** [marketing page](https://www.zoom.com/en/products/appointment-scheduler/), [start here to setup this feature](https://support.zoom.com/hc/en/article?id=zm_kb&sysparm_article=KB0058092)
* **Salesforce:** marketing pages for their [Gmail and Outlook integrations](https://help.salesforce.com/s/articleView?id=sf.email_int_user_overview.htm&language=en_US&type=5) and [Scheduler](https://www.salesforce.com/products/platform/products/salesforce-scheduler/)add-on, starting points to setup [the inbox integrations](https://help.salesforce.com/s/articleView?id=sf.inbox_client_insert_availability.htm&type=5) and [Scheduler](https://help.salesforce.com/s/articleView?id=sf.ls_help_learning_map.htm&type=5)
* **HubSpot:** [marketing page](https://www.hubspot.com/products/sales/schedule-meeting), [start here to setup this feature](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/meetings-tool/create-and-edit-scheduling-pages?hubs_content=knowledge.hubspot.com%2Fmeetings-tool%2Fcreate-and-edit-scheduling-pages&hubs_content-cta=kb-breadcrumbs__item)

**My Experience**  
Calendly is the easiest to work with on both sides (i.e. a person sharing availability and booking a meeting). Prior to Calendly, I used Google Appointment Scheduling, and decided to move away from it based on issues with certain IT environments and browser extensions blocking embedded pages on my website.

**TLDR:** Start using a tool to share times you can meet. Stop sharing times via email.
