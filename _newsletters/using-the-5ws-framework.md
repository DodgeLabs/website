The same 5Ws that we learned in primary school is a helpful framework to use in digital transformation initiatives. When architecting business processes and systems, it is useful to learn about an environment and can make others question assumptions that have been made.

As a quick refresher, the 5Ws are:

1. Who
2. What
3. Where
4. When
5. Why

Even though it seems overly simplistic, there are a variety of ways that the 5Ws can be applied within an organization:

* Defining problems
* Gathering requirements
* Designing solutions
* Identifying opportunities
* Gaining insights in a sales process
* Developing go to market strategies

One of the strongest reasons to use the 5Ws is that it is universally understood by stakeholders across industries, demographics, and experience levels. It also is comprehensive in its scope, scales well for different sized features and projects, and is easily adopted.

This is the first post of a series that will focus on each of the Ws and dive into topics to consider when defining problems, gathering requirements, and designing solutions. This series will conclude with [an example business case](/newsletter/using-the-5ws-framework-example-business-case) to consider and [a set of questions to ask when presented with that case](/newsletter/using-the-5ws-framework-responding-to-the-example-business-case).

**TLDR:** 5Ws is easy to adopt and apply to practically any digital transformation initiative.
